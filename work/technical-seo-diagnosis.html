Google couldn't see 85% of the site.

A window and door company kept being told it needed more content. That was never the problem. The problem was that Google could not see most of the site it already had.

For a regional window & door company ranking 1,600+ keywords but leaking most of its own visibility.

The hidden problem

On paper the site looked healthy: it ranked over 1,600 keywords and its on-page score was 97 out of 100. But the sitemap declared only 32 URLs while the site actually had 226. Google was being told most of the site did not exist.

85%

of the company's own pages were effectively invisible to Google Not because of missing content. Because the map that tells Google what exists was pointing at a fraction of it.

The other leaks

Alongside the sitemap, the site had zero structured data across every page, while its competitors had it. And it ranked #4 for its single most valuable search term but was missing from the local map pack, which drives three to five times the leads of an organic listing. That one gap was the largest revenue lever in the whole engagement.

The part most audits get wrong

The crawl flagged 30+ types of issues. Most SEO reports hand the client that whole list and call it thorough. I did the opposite: I triaged it.

Fixed first — 80% of the impact
  • Rebuilt the sitemap to include all 226 pages
  • Added the sitemap directive to robots.txt
  • Deployed LocalBusiness, Service and FAQ schema
  • One template fix that resolved 13 blog issues at once
  • Trimmed titles on the top ranking pages
Deliberately skipped — noise
  • Render-blocking flags on a 97/100 site
  • Cosmetic title-attribute warnings
  • False-positive "thin content" on visual pages
  • URL-format flags that were already fine
  • Deprecated-tag warnings Google ignores
Spending a quarter of a small budget on 5% of the growth is how most SEO money gets wasted. The judgment is knowing which five things matter and having the discipline to skip the other thirty.
